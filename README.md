# Flexible Inflation Targeting in India: Whether Targets Were Met

## Project Description
This project examines whether India's Reserve Bank has actually met its own inflation target since adopting Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) in 2016. Using a decade of monthly CPI data (2016–2025), the analysis tracks how closely inflation tracked the RBI's 4% ± 2% band year by year, identifies the periods where the target was breached and why, and benchmarks these findings against the RBI's own August 2025 discussion paper on the FIT framework's future.

*Group Assignment (GIPE M.Sc. Agribusiness Economics), under the guidance of Prof. Ranjit Kumar Pattnaik.*

## Objective
To evaluate the effectiveness of India's Flexible Inflation Targeting framework (2016–2025) by analyzing CPI inflation trends against the RBI's 4% ± 2% target band, and to assess these findings against RBI's own recent policy review.

## Methodology
- Compiled and tabulated monthly CPI inflation data (2016–2025)
- Built a frequency distribution of months falling below (<2%), within (2–6%), and above (>6%) the RBI's target band
- Analyzed year-by-year inflation drivers (monsoon, crude oil prices, pandemic disruptions, geopolitical shocks, etc.)
- Compared the pre-FIT (pre-2016) and post-FIT (2016–2025) inflation regimes
- Reviewed findings against RBI's August 2025 discussion paper on the FIT framework

## Key Findings
- 2016–2019: Inflation mostly stayed within the 2–6% target range
- 2020–2022: Sustained breach of the upper 6% limit, driven by pandemic supply shocks, and later the Russia-Ukraine war and global commodity price shocks
- 2023–2024: Inflation moderated back within target for most months
- 2025 (through April): Inflation stayed comfortably within target, at 3.3–4.3%
- Average inflation fell from ~6.8% pre-FIT to ~4.9% under FIT (2016–2025), indicating improved price stability
